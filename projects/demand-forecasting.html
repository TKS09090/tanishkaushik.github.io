THE BUSINESS QUESTION

What is the likely weekly outbound unit demand over the next 12 weeks?

The project analyses a 78-week demand history for a fictional e-commerce fulfilment operation and develops a variable short-term forecast to support fulfilment and operations planning.

Project objectives

  • Describe the historical demand profile and variability.
  • Compare three simple forecasting benchmarks on a holdout window.
  • Select a method based on observed validation performance.
  • Produce a dated 12-week projection and decision-ready dashboard.

ANALYTICAL APPROACH

Forecasting and validation workflow

01

Profile demand

Review 78 consecutive weekly observations and calculate central tendency, dispersion and range.

02

Define holdout

Reserve the final 12 historical weeks (27 Jul–12 Oct 2026) for rolling one-week-ahead validation.

03

Compare methods

Evaluate naïve, four-week moving average and 52-week seasonal naïve forecasts.

04

Generate outlook

Use the best-performing holdout benchmark to produce a 12-week dated projection.

SELECTED FORECAST LOGIC

52-week seasonal naïve

Forecast(t) = Actual Demand(t − 52 weeks)

Each forecast week uses demand from the corresponding week one year earlier. It was selected because it had the lowest error on this specific holdout, not because it is guaranteed to remain best.

MODEL PERFORMANCE

Holdout comparison

All three methods were tested against the same final 12 historical weeks. Lower MAE and RMSE indicate lower forecast error.

VALIDATION INSIGHT

Seasonal naïve had the lowest error on the test window

It reduced MAE by approximately 11.4% versus naïve and 18.1% versus the four-week moving average. These comparisons apply to this holdout only.

INTERPRETATION

Model selection is evidence-led, not absolute

The result supports using seasonal naïve as a planning baseline for this case. Performance should be monitored and revalidated as new actual demand becomes available.

SCOPE & LIMITATIONS

What this analysis does — and does not — claim

Included in this version

  • Historical demand profile and variability.
  • Three benchmark methods and 12-week holdout metrics.
  • 12-week projection and calendar-month forecast mix.
  • Formula-linked Excel dashboard.

Outside the project scope

  • Workforce, labour-cost and capacity planning.
  • Backlog, utilisation and on-time dispatch analysis.
  • Supplier, inventory and transport optimisation.
  • Live external data integration or causal driver modelling.
